CURRENT GOAL
------------
Reach a premium stylized 2.5D mobile-shooter visual quality comparable in polish/readability to games such as Zombie Waves, without copying their assets or designs.

Chosen production strategy:
3D master assets -> rig/animations -> orthographic 8-direction renders -> high-resolution transparent frames -> downsample -> exact game sprite contract -> QA -> final atlas.

This replaces the idea of independently generating hundreds of AI sprite frames, because independent generations do not preserve character identity, proportions, equipment and animation continuity reliably enough.

GRAPHICS DIRECTION
------------------
Rex identity:
- futuristic armored ranger
- athletic, compact, readable silhouette
- enclosed helmet
- cyan visor/energy accents
- dark navy / near-black armor
- restrained orange accents
- tactical cape
- futuristic assault rifle as major silhouette element

Rendering target:
- stylized premium rather than retro pixel-art
- strong silhouette at phone scale
- clean PBR-like material separation in master renders
- controlled lighting and shadows
- high-resolution master renders (target around 512x512) before final reduction
- transparent background for character frames
- consistent orthographic/isometric presentation

Rex and enemies alone are not sufficient for the target quality. Final polish must also include environments, shadows, weapon VFX, muzzle flashes, projectiles, impacts, explosions, hit feedback, particles, UI feedback and strong mobile readability.

10. Produce/test hit.

QUALITY GATES
-------------
A master character is NOT approved merely because it looks impressive in a large preview.

Rex must pass:
- recognizable silhouette at gameplay scale
- same identity from all 8 directions
- stable proportions
- clean helmet silhouette
- readable rifle silhouette
- acceptable hands and weapon separation
- no obvious melted/intersecting geometry
- rig deforms correctly
- animation loops are stable
- feet do not visibly slide during stationary animations
- no large pivot drift between frames
- cape does not destroy silhouette/readability
- cyan/orange accents survive final downsampling
- final 96x96 result remains readable on a phone

RULES / DECISIONS TO PRESERVE
-----------------------------
- The original user-created PixelLab GIF is Rex, not another character.
- PixelLab can remain useful for concepts/experiments/imports, but it is no longer the sole production method for hundreds of final character frames.
- Do not treat AI-generated labelled presentation boards as production sprite sheets.
- Do not generate 232 independent Rex images and assume consistency.
- Build one excellent Rex master first, then derive animation frames from it.
- Prefer fixing errors at the 3D-master stage instead of repairing hundreds of rendered frames later.
- Optimize for actual Android gameplay readability, not maximum microscopic detail.
